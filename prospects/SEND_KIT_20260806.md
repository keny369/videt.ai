## 1. How I would run it

**Email body carries the finding. PDF carries the proof. Video carries the shock.**

Do not attach the PDF to a cold email. Attachments to unknown recipients hurt deliverability and get quarantined by exactly the kind of business filter these firms run. Instead:

1. **Email**: three short paragraphs, one measured count, one link.
2. **Link goes to the video**, not the PDF. The video is the thing that makes them reply, and a link is harmless.
3. **PDF is offered, not sent**: "I've put the counts on one page, want me to send it?" That converts a read into a reply, which is what you actually want.
4. **Send the PDF on reply.** Now it is a warm attachment to someone expecting it.

The exception is Metropole and Cate Bakos, who are sophisticated enough to want the document immediately. For those two, link the PDF as well.

**Sequence per prospect:** email → reply → PDF → call booked → 90-second live demo on the call → offer.

**One video link per prospect.** Each is your shared opener plus their 40-second closer, so each needs its own upload. Unlisted YouTube or Loom. Do not use a public link.

**Which emotion have I uncovered?** Wrong → reputation. Missing → visibility. Losing → competitive position. Everything else follows.

### Three refinements

**1. Buyers Advocate does not fit cleanly, and that is worth knowing.** They are cited constantly and recognised never: the most-quoted source in the study, and 5 of 8 direct questions returned "I don't know". That is not Wrong, not Missing, not Losing. It is closer to **being used without credit**. Do not add a fourth light, the model's strength is that it has three. But file it under Missing and have the sharper line ready: *"They're reading you constantly and they still can't tell a buyer who you are."*

**2. The colours fight you slightly.** Green normally means good, and you are using it for Losing. On a phone that is one beat of friction you do not need. Consider red / amber / black, or drop the colours and keep the three words. The words are doing the work.

**3. Do not over-promise on Missing.** Your answer to "can this be fixed?" is *"Yes. Almost every issue we've found has a path to improvement."* True for Wrong, which is correctable at source. **Weaker for Missing**, where the honest position is the two-clock model: retrieval-layer fixes propagate in days to weeks and we re-measure them, but what the models remember from training changes slowly and nobody can schedule it. Say that out loud on absence cases. It costs you nothing and it is the sentence that makes the rest of your discipline credible.

### The line to have ready before you send anything

The question that decides one sale versus two is **"so what do I actually fix?"** Your medicine analogy handles it well. Have it word-perfect before Metropole reads his report, because he is exactly the person who will push.
