# Chapter 19 — Engineering Ethics and Professional Conduct

## 1. Purpose

This chapter defines the professional standards expected of every individual contributing to the F1 platform.

Engineering excellence depends not only upon technical competence but also upon integrity, accountability, transparency and respect for the engineering process.

The long-term quality of the platform is determined as much by engineering behaviour as by engineering skill.

Every contributor SHALL uphold these standards.

---

# 2. Scope

This chapter applies to:

- software engineers;
- architects;
- engineering managers;
- reviewers;
- DevOps engineers;
- QA engineers;
- contractors;
- AI-assisted engineering workflows;
- technical leadership.

These standards apply regardless of employment status or seniority.

---

# 3. Professional Principles

Every engineer SHALL demonstrate:

- honesty;
- technical integrity;
- accountability;
- professional curiosity;
- respect for evidence;
- commitment to continuous improvement.

Engineering decisions SHALL always prioritise the long-term health of the platform.

---

# 4. Integrity

Engineering integrity requires that contributors:

- accurately represent implementation status;
- report defects honestly;
- acknowledge uncertainty;
- avoid overstating confidence;
- document known limitations;
- correct discovered mistakes promptly.

Knowingly concealing engineering defects is prohibited.

---

# 5. Accountability

Every engineer is accountable for:

- the quality of their work;
- adherence to the Product Specification;
- compliance with the Engineering Manual;
- maintaining repository quality;
- supporting production stability.

Approval by another engineer does not remove individual responsibility.

---

# 6. Evidence-Based Engineering

Engineering discussions SHALL be based upon evidence.

Evidence MAY include:

- Product Specification;
- Engineering Manual;
- ADRs;
- benchmarks;
- automated tests;
- production metrics;
- incident analysis.

Opinion SHALL NOT override objective engineering evidence.

---

# 7. Communication

Engineering communication SHALL be:

- respectful;
- concise;
- technically precise;
- constructive;
- professionally written.

Disagreement SHALL address technical issues rather than individuals.

---

# 8. Continuous Learning

Every engineer SHOULD pursue continuous improvement through:

- code review;
- retrospective analysis;
- architectural discussion;
- technical reading;
- experimentation within governed environments;
- mentoring.

Knowledge sharing strengthens the engineering organisation.

---

# 9. Production Responsibility

Engineers SHALL recognise that production software affects real users.

Engineering decisions SHALL consider:

- reliability;
- security;
- operational support;
- maintainability;
- recoverability.

Production incidents SHALL be investigated to improve the platform rather than assign blame.

---

# 10. AI-Assisted Engineering

AI SHALL be used responsibly.

Engineers SHALL:

- review AI-generated output;
- verify correctness;
- preserve traceability;
- avoid delegating engineering judgement to AI.

Responsibility for AI-assisted work remains with the human engineer.

---

# 11. Conflicts of Interest

Engineering decisions SHALL be made in the interests of:

- product correctness;
- architectural integrity;
- repository quality;
- operational stability.

Personal preference SHALL NOT override established engineering governance.

---

# 12. Engineering Leadership

Engineering leaders SHALL:

- model repository standards;
- encourage technical excellence;
- support architectural consistency;
- mentor less experienced contributors;
- maintain a culture of evidence-based engineering.

Leadership SHALL reinforce governance rather than circumvent it.

---

# 13. Handling Mistakes

Engineering mistakes are expected.

Failure to acknowledge or correct them is not.

When defects are discovered engineers SHALL:

1. identify the defect;
2. assess impact;
3. communicate appropriately;
4. correct the issue;
5. document lessons where beneficial.

Continuous improvement is preferred over fault allocation.

---

# 14. Review Conduct

Review discussions SHALL:

- remain technical;
- avoid personal criticism;
- explain reasoning;
- cite authoritative sources;
- seek the best engineering outcome.

Review is collaborative engineering.

---

# 15. Repository Stewardship

Every contributor shares responsibility for repository quality.

Engineers SHOULD leave the repository in a better state than they found it.

Examples include:

- improving documentation;
- simplifying implementation;
- correcting inaccuracies;
- improving tests;
- reducing technical debt.

---

# 16. Anti-Patterns

The following behaviours are prohibited.

- Concealing known defects.
- Ignoring repository governance.
- Misrepresenting implementation status.
- Accepting work without review.
- Circumventing architectural authority.
- Blaming individuals instead of investigating systems.
- Allowing schedule pressure to justify reduced engineering quality.
- Delegating professional responsibility to AI.

---

# 17. Professional Conduct Checklist

Every engineer SHOULD regularly ask:

- Have I implemented the Specification faithfully?
- Have I preserved architectural integrity?
- Have I documented significant decisions?
- Have I improved the repository?
- Have I communicated honestly?
- Have I left sufficient evidence for future engineers?
- Would I be comfortable defending this implementation during a production incident review?

---

# 18. Compliance

Professional conduct is an engineering quality concern.

Persistent disregard for these standards SHALL be addressed through the project's engineering governance process.

These standards apply equally to technical leadership and individual contributors.
