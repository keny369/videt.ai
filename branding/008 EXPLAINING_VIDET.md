# Explaining VIDET — the plain-language kit

Status: Operational messaging aid, 29 July 2026. Written after the Mark Thurman call (29 Jul) exposed the gap: VIDET kept being filed under "AEO". Conforms to `branding/006 BRAND_PACK.md` voice quick-card and the claim rules in `investor/30_DAY_REVENUE_PLAN.md` Appendix C. For conversation, not for publication — publication copy still comes from `branding/001 WEBSITE_STRATEGY.md`.

## The rule that fixes the problem

**"We measure, we collect evidence, we audit" answers "how are you different from AEO?" — it does NOT answer "what is this?"**

Two questions, two scripts. Never use the differentiator as the description; it's abstract, it's about you rather than them, and it invites the very category argument you want to avoid. Lead with the description. Reach for the differentiator only when someone hands you a label.

---

## 1. The description (default answer to "what do you do?")

**The mystery-shopper handle — use this first, every time:**

> "You know how big retailers pay mystery shoppers to walk in pretending to be a customer, to find out what actually gets said when the boss isn't there? I do that with AI. When someone asks ChatGPT 'who's the best {their trade} in {their city}', I'm the one asking — around 22 different ways, over and over — and I write down exactly what it says about you. Then I hand you the recording and the ten things to fix."

Why it works: mystery shopping is a service Australians already understand and already pay for. It carries the method inside it with no technical words, and it sets the expectation of a *report* rather than a *retainer*.

**The one-liner (badge version):**
> "I find out what AI tells your customers about you — and what to fix when it's wrong."

**The reason-they-need-it line:**
> "Your customers are asking machines who to hire. You've never seen the answers."

**The reframe that ends the category confusion (use as the follow-up):**
> "Search engines gave people a list and they picked. AI gives them one answer — a name — and they ring it. **There's no page two.** Either it says your name or it doesn't."

## 2. The differentiator (only when someone says "oh, that's AEO/SEO")

Never say "no it isn't." Concede the adjacency, then take the higher ground.

**Business-owner version (one line):**
> "AEO is the treatment. I'm the diagnosis — and the proof it worked."

**Full version:**
> "That's the fixing half, and yeah, we end up there. But everyone selling AEO is doing surgery without an X-ray — nobody's checked what the machines actually say about you first, and nobody goes back afterwards to prove it changed. That's my bit. The fixes come out of the evidence, not out of a template."

**Marketer/agency version (market-structure argument — use with Mark):**
> "AEO is a crowded commodity: everyone promises visibility, nobody proves it. Position us there and we're in a price fight with fifty vendors. Position us as the audit that comes first, and we're the thing they all need and none of them do. Measurement is the wedge — optimisation is what follows it, only where the evidence supports it."

**The killer question if someone is already paying an AEO vendor:**
> "Fair enough — what did they show you it was like before they started, and what does it look like now?" (Almost nobody can answer. That gap is the sale.)

## 3. Per-persona openers

**High-ticket trade:**
> "When someone's about to spend forty grand on floors, a lot of them now ask ChatGPT who to use before they ring anyone. I find out what it says when they ask about {city} {trade} — and whether your name comes up at all."

**Regulated professional:**
> "People are asking ChatGPT 'is this firm any good, are they actually registered tax agents?' It answers with real confidence, and sometimes it's wrong about you. I go and find out exactly what it's telling them."

**Agency / consultant:**
> "Your clients are starting to ask you what ChatGPT says about them. Right now you'd answer with a few prompts and a screenshot. I give you the method, the counts and a client-ready report — with a re-measure that proves what changed."

## 4. What VIDET is not (say these out loud — refusing things builds trust)

- Not blog-writing or content production.
- Not keyword or "AI phrase" stuffing — the machines themselves say it doesn't work.
- Not a promise you'll appear. Nobody can promise that, and anyone who does is guessing.
- Not a monitoring subscription (not yet — the platform is in development).
- Not a score out of 100. Counts, dates and screenshots only.

## 5. The two-sentence version for when someone is busy

> "Your customers ask AI who to hire, and it gives them a name. I find out whether it's yours, what it gets wrong about you, and who it recommends instead — then hand you ten things to fix and come back in 90 days to show what changed."

## 6. Self-test before any new explanation goes into use

1. Would a 55-year-old flooring contractor repeat it back correctly?
2. Does it contain a word from the banned list (SEO, GEO, AI visibility, platform-as-product, optimisation, algorithm, schema, entity)?
3. Is it about *their* business, or about *our* method?
4. Could every claim in it be screenshotted?

If it fails 1 or 3, it's a differentiator masquerading as a description — go back to the mystery shopper.
