### Finding your live error before you record (do this first, allow 10 minutes)

You need one confident, checkable, false statement on screen. Run these in **separate fresh chats** (memory off, temporary chat), in rough order of hit rate. Screenshot everything, including the ones that come back clean.

**Probe 1 — the source trap. Highest yield, and it is the Clutch pattern.**
> `Who are the best AI consultants in Melbourne?`

then, in the same chat:

> `How do you know about these companies? Please list the specific pages or sources for each one.`

Open every URL it gives you. The failure you are hunting is a **real page that does not contain what the machine says it contains**. This is the single most common error and the most damning on camera, because the link works, which is exactly why nobody checks.

**Probe 2 — entity conflation on your own name.** Lee Powell is a common name (there is a Welsh footballer, among others), so the odds of a merge are high.
> `Who is Lee Powell of Lumen & Lever in Melbourne, and what is his professional background?`

Hunt for: a career that is not yours, a wrong location, a business you do not own, or your Scrivener work attributed to someone else.

**Probe 3 — hard facts on a real business** (yours, or a client's with written permission).
> `What services does {business} offer, where are they located, and what is their phone number?`

Hunt for: stale address, dead number, a service they dropped, a service they never offered.

**Probe 4 — the credentials question.**
> `Is {business} licensed and registered? What are their credentials?`

Hunt for: an invented licence number, a registration they do not hold, or confident reassurance with no basis.

**Probe 5 — the control, if the first four come back clean.** Ask about a business that does not exist, using a plausible name for the category and suburb.
> `Tell me about Harper & Lane Accountants in Carlton. What do they specialise in?`

If it describes them, you have a fabrication on film in one take. Nobody real is harmed and the mechanism is undeniable. Keep this as the backup demo, not the main one: a viewer can dismiss it with "well, my business exists", whereas a wrong fact about a real business is the one that makes them think *what is it saying about me*.

**What makes an error usable on camera:** it is specific (a number, a name, an address, a credential), it is checkable in one click while the camera rolls, and it is stated without hedging. Skip anything vague or heavily qualified; "may offer" is not a finding.

**Verify on camera.** The demo is twice as strong if you open the source or the real page in the next tab and show the truth beside the claim. That is the whole product in fifteen seconds.

**Production rules**
- Real screens only. Never re-shoot to get a "better" answer, and never stage one.
- If the live answer happens to include you, keep rolling and pivot: *"there I am today. Ask it again tomorrow and it moves, which is exactly why one answer proves nothing and I count them."*
- Client reports appear on screen only with written permission, and are introduced as client work.
- QuickTime plus a phone mic is enough. In a feed full of polished AI hype, an unpolished founder screen recording is the pattern interrupt. Do not add music.
- One take is fine. Do not caption over the silence.
