## Appendix D — Protocol v0 (the assessment specification)

**1. Question set (per business):** 20–25 buyer-intent questions built from the template: {best | recommend | who should I use for | top} × {service} × {suburb | city | "near me" phrasing}, plus 3–5 business-specific questions (their highest-value service lines) and 2 control questions (a category they *don't* serve — calibrates the assistant's willingness to name anyone).

**2. Assistants:** ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Claude, Perplexity — consumer surfaces, queried manually, fresh conversations, no personalisation logged in where avoidable.

**3. Sampling:** each question ≥3 times per assistant (fresh session each time), spread across ≥2 days. Record every run — the *variance* is itself a finding customers understand instantly.

**4. Capture rules:** screenshot every response; log date, time, assistant, model/version where visible; verbatim answer text into the run sheet. Evidence folder per client. Nothing is reported that isn't captured.

**5. Findings taxonomy (count everything, score nothing):**
- Present / absent (per question, per assistant, per run)
- Cited (named with a source/link) vs uncited mention
- Wrong fact (address, services, hours, names — each logged verbatim)
- Competitor named (who, how often, in what terms)
- Consistency (same question, different answers — note the spread)

**6. Report skeleton (template v1, VIDET identity):** (1) The question your customers are asking — one page, their own vertical's questions; (2) What the machines said — the counts, with 3–4 verbatim excerpts and screenshots; (3) What they got wrong — the facts table; (4) Who they named instead — attributed, factual; (5) The ten fixes, prioritised, in plain English, each with "what" and "why it matters"; (6) What happens next — the 90-day re-measure. Every claim traceable to a captured run. Closing line of every report: *"Every observation in this report is screenshot-backed and dated. Ask us for any of them."*

**7. Practice-run register:** 0a sprungfloors.au (full) · 0b jafloors.com.au (full) · 0c whollistica.com (full) · 0d lumenandlever.com (quick-pass) · 0e leepowell.com (quick-pass). **Ownership (corrected 28 Jul):** 0a–0c are CLIENT businesses (long-standing clients of Lee's — run with their permission, disclosed as client work in every use); only 0d–0e are Lee's own. Purpose: time the protocol, shake down question sets per vertical, produce two disclosed case studies. Never counted as customers.

**8. Time budget:** first full run ≤8 hours; by run five, target ≤4 hours. Whatever exceeds the budget is the September automation shopping list.

## Appendix F — The demo script (the sale, 90 seconds)

1. "Can I show you something about your business? Ninety seconds." Open ChatGPT (then Gemini) live, in front of them.
2. Ask: "Who's the best [their service] in [their suburb/city]?" and one buying-intent variant.
3. **Silence while they read.** They will be absent, misdescribed, or beaten by a named competitor.
4. If they *do* appear well: "That's one answer, today, phrased one way. The assessment asks 22 real buyer questions across four assistants, repeatedly. Want to know if it holds?"
5. One line of category education, maximum: "This is where customers increasingly start. Not your Google ranking — what the machines *say* when asked."
6. The offer: "So that's one answer, on one day, phrased one way. The assessment asks 22 real buyer questions across four assistants, over and over, and I hand you the counts, the screenshots, and the ten fixes that matter — plain English, within five business days of payment. Founding price is $990, or $1,490 with a 90-day re-measure that shows what changed. I can start this week."
7. The walkthrough close (end of every walkthrough call): "You now know more about how the machines see you than almost any owner in your trade. Two asks before we wrap. First — in 90 days I can re-run every question and put your before-and-after on paper; want me to book it now? Second — who are two businesses you know that should see what the machines say about them?"

## Appendix I — Checkpoints and the Pivot Protocol

**1. Checkpoint 1 (Day 10):** ≥30 pitches, ≥8 demos done/booked, ≥2 paid. Short on pitches → the calendar is the problem; fix blocks. Short on demos → the message is the problem; sharpen the hook line. Short on paid → watch two more demos closely before touching price.

**2. Checkpoint 2 (Day 14):** ≥4 external paid; price holding ≥$990; demo→sale ≥1-in-3. Adjust **one** variable maximum. Never two at once — you won't know which one worked.

**3. Pivot Protocol (only if the Day-21 kill line trips):**
1. Stop pitching for 48 hours. Book 5 interviews with people who said no ($50 gift cards if needed). One question: what would have made it a yes — price, proof, urgency, or "don't care"?
2. **Price** → test A$490 assessment-only on 10 pitches (the floor test; below this the business doesn't work anyway).
3. **Proof** → lead every pitch with the best case study instead of the demo.
4. **Urgency/awareness** → the vertical is wrong; swap to the vertical with the best demo-reaction data from Weeks 1–3.
5. **"Don't care" across two verticals** → a real market answer. Take it to the DE plan's stop/pivot gates (direct-brand vs practitioner channel) rather than burning month two on the same motion.
