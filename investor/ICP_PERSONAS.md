# VIDET — Ideal Customer Profiles (3 personas)

Operational targeting document, 29 July 2026. Derived from `investor/VIDET_DISCIPLINED_ENTREPRENEURSHIP_PLAN.md` Steps 1–5, `investor/QUESTION_BANK.md` verticals, `operations/AUTHORITY_ROADMAP.md` customer tiers, and the Day 3 named-100 mix in `investor/30_DAY_REVENUE_PLAN.md` (~40 trades / ~30 professionals / ~30 partner-channel). Personas 1 and 2 are the **cash-now concierge** buyers; persona 3 is the **platform-era beachhead hypothesis** and is gated — see the warning under it.

## What makes any business a fit (the seven-point screen)

1. **Bought by recommendation, not browsing.** Buyers ask "who should I use for X in Y?" — the archetype A question. If customers arrive by tender, contract or shelf, we are the wrong product.
2. **One job or client is worth more than the fee.** A single recovered enquiry must repay A$990–1,490 several times over.
3. **Geographic.** The business serves a place; machines must resolve location, and location is where they fail.
4. **Owner-operator decides.** One conversation to yes. No procurement, no committee.
5. **A registerable identity.** ABN, licences, professional registers, manufacturer listings — these are the surfaces machines read, so they are what we can actually fix. A business with regulatory records is *more* fixable than one without.
6. **Reputation-sensitive.** Wrong facts said confidently about them is felt as a threat, not a curiosity.
7. **Reference value.** Will consent to a named before/after case study.

## Persona 1 — The high-ticket specialist trade

**Who.** Owner-led specialist installer, fabricator or builder. 5–50 staff, A$2–20M turnover, one or two metro service areas. Sprung floors, timber flooring, custom joinery, pools, custom builds, commercial fitout, specialist roofing. The kind of business that holds manufacturer authorised-installer status.

**Job size.** A$15k–500k. One recovered job repays the assessment 15–500×. This is the easiest ROI argument in the whole book.

**Why they're ideal.** Their category is *thin* in the machines' evidence — niche installer questions ("who installs sprung floors for dance studios in Melbourne?") often return generic builders or nothing at all, which makes Absence findings dramatic and immediate. They live on referral and reputation, so being unnamed is viscerally understood. Manufacturer listings, state building-authority licences and trade association memberships give a concrete, cheap fix list. Lee has direct network access and two completed practice-run reports in this exact vertical.

**What they buy.** The demo. Show them the answer to their own buyer question and the sale happens in the room. They do not buy "AI strategy" and will switch off if you use the word platform.

**Pain sentence.** *"A customer with a $40,000 job asked ChatGPT who to call, and it named three companies. None of them were you."*

**Where to find them.** Lee's flooring and building network, suppliers and manufacturers (who know every installer), architects and specifiers, trade associations, Scaling members in building/trades.

**Risk.** Least abstractly interested in AI — the 90-second live demo is doing all the work. Also lowest natural digital literacy, so fixes may need doing *for* them (fix-it engagement upsell, A$2,500–5,000).

## Persona 2 — The regulated professional practice

**Who.** Owner-led practice with 1–10 practitioners: accountants and bookkeepers, dentists and orthodontists, cosmetic clinics, vets, physios, commercial lawyers and conveyancers, mortgage and finance brokers, IT providers/MSPs. Metro or large regional.

**Client value.** A$3k–50k lifetime per client, usually recurring. One new client repays the fee many times, and their client base compounds.

**Why they're ideal.** Buyers ask assistants *validation* questions about them by name — "Is {business} any good? Are they registered tax agents?" (archetype E) — which means the machine is already making claims about their legitimacy, sometimes wrongly. That's the sharpest version of the problem we sell. They sit on public registers (TPB, AHPRA, ASIC Financial Advisers, law societies, MFAA/FBAA), so entity resolution and authority verification are concrete and fixable, and Layer 1 of the Readiness Audit produces real findings almost every time. They are the most reputation-sensitive segment on the list: a machine inventing a credential or confusing them with another practice is an urgent problem, not an interesting one.

**What they buy.** Professional risk management. Frame it as what the machine tells prospective clients about their credentials and whether it's accurate — not as marketing.

**Pain sentence.** *"ChatGPT told someone you're not a registered tax agent. You are. It said it anyway, and the person believed it."*

**Where to find them.** Lee's own accountant and lawyer, clinic-adjacent contacts, professional associations, referral chains between professionals (accountants know every SMB in town), Scaling members.

**Risk.** Regulated verticals require the extended Part 11 disclaimer on every report, and AHPRA advertising rules constrain what a clinic may publish — check before recommending review-solicitation fixes.

## Persona 3 — The owner-led agency or independent consultant *(channel — gated)*

**Who.** Owner-led digital agency or established independent consultant carrying 10–50 retained client domains in service categories. AU east coast first, then UK/US. This is the DE plan's beachhead hypothesis and the persona Lee himself occupies through Lumen & Lever.

**Value.** Platform ACV hypothesised at A$6k–15k, plus per-client usage. One sale reaches many businesses — the only persona with real distribution leverage.

**Why they're ideal.** Their clients are already asking them "what does ChatGPT say about us?" and they currently answer with ad-hoc prompts and screenshots. They need a defensible method, staged intervention plans, proof of implementation and client-ready reporting — plus a new recurring revenue line. They are users, implementers and distributors at once.

**⚠️ Two hard constraints — do not breach.**
- **No cross-client surface exists.** On the frozen baseline each client is its own Organization; the practitioner is invited into each; there is no roll-up, consolidated export or portfolio dashboard, and none may be implied. v1 motion is per-client Organizations with the partner assembling portfolio reporting outside the product. A Partner Portfolio Console is a future owner decision — **never promise it, never price it.**
- **This persona is a hypothesis, not a conclusion.** The DE plan gates it: confirm agencies as beachhead only if ≥5 sign paid pilots or LOIs with price and start date, *and* the per-client workflow survives contact. If partners abandon at multi-login friction, that is a pivot signal toward direct brands.

**Risk.** They may resell rather than buy, which changes economics and raises IP exposure — partner terms need confidentiality and non-circumvention before anything deepens (Appendix G.4). Also the slowest to cash: personas 1 and 2 pay this month; this one is a 2026–27 motion.

## Who is NOT an ICP (say no, fast)

- **Tender/contract-won businesses** — government suppliers, wholesalers, B2B manufacturers whose buyers never ask for a recommendation.
- **Pure e-commerce and product brands** — assistants recommend products there, a different question shape and a different method than ours.
- **Enterprise with procurement** — long cycles, low reference value, wrong price point (DE plan scores it low on access and budget).
- **Pre-revenue startups and new businesses** — no NAP footprint, no registers, no reviews: nothing to measure and nothing to fix.
- **Career-switching beginners and hobby operators** — explicitly excluded by the DE plan segment screen.
- **Anyone wanting guaranteed rankings** — refuse-to-sell; they will be unhappy whatever we deliver.

## The qualifying questions (use on any call)

1. "When you win a new customer, how did they usually find you?" → must be recommendation/referral/search, not tender.
2. "What's a typical job or client worth to you over a year?" → must clear A$5k comfortably.
3. "Which suburbs or areas do you actually serve?" → confirms geography and gives the wrong-area control.
4. "Are you the person who'd decide on something like this?" → owner-operator test.
5. "Have you ever asked ChatGPT what it says about your business?" → if they've tried and been unsettled, they're already sold; if they've never thought about it, run the demo.

## Priority for the founding twenty

Weight the cohort roughly **8 trades / 8 professionals / 4 partner-channel**, matching the Day 3 named-100 mix. Trades give the fastest yes and the most dramatic findings; professionals give the best recurring-value story and the most fixable register surfaces; the four partner-channel places exist to test persona 3 cheaply, before committing the platform roadmap to it.
