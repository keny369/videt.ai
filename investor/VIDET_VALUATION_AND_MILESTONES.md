The 50–55% top row is the base rate for pre-revenue solo ventures, not a judgement. The realistic "well executed + deep pockets" answer is row three, with row four reachable because Profound proved the seat price. **The flank exists because the wave is monitoring-first: nobody owns verified intervention outcomes — the "did the fix actually work" layer.**

### Two framing corrections

1. **Proof, not connections, is the scarce input.** Sequoia/Lightspeed/Kleiner are already deployed in this category and hunting. Five paying pilots + retention + a published, scrutinise-able methodology brings capital to you; without proof, connections produce coffee meetings, not term sheets. Networking hours before the pilot cohort exists are mis-spent.
2. **Company value ≠ founder value.** Bootstrapped row two at ~90–100% ownership is nearly all yours (A$5–20M) — arguably the best risk-adjusted personal outcome. The venture path to row three costs ~45–60% dilution: 25–35% of US$80–300M = **US$25–100M personal**, but converts row-two safety into row-one risk (funded companies cannot gear down; investors force the swing). Raise only to chase rows three/four, and raise *after* pilots, when it's cheap.

## 4. Exit bands (the stated goal)

| Band | Value | What it takes | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Capability tuck-in** | **US$15–50M** | US$1.5–3M ARR is enough *if the asset is strategic*: ~50–100 quality accounts, 500+ businesses under observation, 200+ measured lifecycles, methodology that survives diligence. Acquirer (Ahrefs, Similarweb, HubSpot, Sprinklr, agency holdco) buys the capability + dataset + founder, not the P&L. **Rational primary target for a solo founder** — nearly all of $20–40M beats a third of $100M risk-adjusted. | ~2.5–3 years, little/no dilution |
| **Real strategic exit** | **US$50–150M** | US$5–10M ARR at 80%+ growth, NRR 115%+, 150–400 accounts with mid-market weight, category-distinct position intact ("the verification and outcome layer"). 8–15× forward ARR. | ~4–5 years; likely 1–2 funding rounds |
| **Premium exit** | **US$150–400M** | US$15–30M ARR, or smaller ARR + an auction between two strategics needing the checkbox in the same quarter. Not planned for — stayed positioned for. | Window-dependent (2027–2029) |

**Backwards math:** strategic buyers pay ~6–12× ARR for growth SaaS → divide target exit by ten for required ARR — *unless selling the dataset*, where lifecycle density and logo quality substitute for revenue. That substitution is why the intervention-verification wedge was chosen.

## 5. The two numbers

- **A$500k ARR with 85% retention** — a real company; every later option (raise, bootstrap, sell) stays open.
- **US$2–3M ARR + 200 completed measured lifecycles by 2028** — buyable, inside the consolidation window, with dilution still near zero.

## 6. Standing warning

Don't run the company *visibly* for exit — acquirers smell it and discount it. The behaviours that maximise a tuck-in offer (dataset density, methodology credibility, retention) are identical to those that build a durable business. **Build as if forever; structure as if selling next quarter** — clean entity, IP assigned into it, evidence/data rights in every pilot contract, provider-ToS position understood.
